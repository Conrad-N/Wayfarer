# 04 — Salvage and derelict ships

## The part kit

Every ship in the game, derelict or player-owned, is built from a kit of parts. Each
part is one `.glb` generated by a script in `tools/blender/`, carrying:

- a low-poly mesh with a convex collision shape,
- `SOCKET_*` empties: where other parts attach,
- `CUT_*` empties: where the cutter can sever it (usually one per socket, just inboard),
- `HAZARD_<kind>_<n>` empties: where volatiles live,
- metadata (`get_meta("extras")`): `part_kind`, `mass_kg`, `value_cr`, `material`,
  `volume_m3`, `thickness_mm`.

Part kinds for the first kit (target 30+ parts across these kinds):

| kind | examples | notes |
|---|---|---|
| hull | segments in S/M/L radius, straight and tapered | cheap material, bulky |
| cap | nose cones, tail plates, bridge modules | often contain sensors, value |
| tank | propellant, oxidiser, water, in several sizes | fuel hazard when not empty |
| engine | chemical, ion, in sizes | high value, heavy, awkward shape |
| reactor | fission core, RTG | highest value, power hazard, deep inside |
| radiator | panels, fins | fragile, coolant hazard, cheap, easy to reach |
| cargo | pods, racks | may contain goods |
| hab | crew modules, airlocks | pressure hazard, medium value |
| mast | sensor booms, antennae, struts | thin, light, cut points along length |
| plating | armour, panels over other parts | must be removed to reach what is beneath |

## Ship graph

A ship is a graph: parts are nodes, socket-to-socket connections are edges. Each
edge has a cut point. The graph is data (`ShipGraph`, a `RefCounted`), separate from
the Godot nodes that display it, so it can be generated, saved, and tested headless.

M2 implementation: `PartDefinition` resources hold physical properties and imported
markers; `ShipPart` holds identity, assembly pose, condition, and scan state.
`ShipGraph` validates parts and socket size/occupancy, stores edges by stable ID,
and returns sorted connected components. Multiple connections between the same
parts are allowed through different sockets; every connection must be cut before
the part is freed. Cutting an edge in a loop need not separate anything yet.

## Generator

`DerelictGenerator.generate(seed, class_name) -> ShipGraph`.

1. Pick a class template: hauler, tug, tanker, liner, patrol, survey. A template gives
   a spine length, allowed part kinds per position, and typical hazards.
2. Grow the spine: caps at both ends, hull segments between, sized by class.
3. Attach: for each free socket, roll a part kind allowed there, check it fits (no
   overlap with existing parts using bounding boxes), attach or leave open.
4. Bury value: reactors and engines go inboard or aft; plating goes over valuable
   parts with probability by class.
5. Age it: mark some tanks as still full (hazard), some compartments as pressurised,
   some power lines live. Set condition per part (affects value).
6. Result: a `ShipGraph` with a seed. The same seed always gives the same ship.

A large hand-made library can be added later as templates with fixed graphs; it uses
the same kit and the same runtime.

## Runtime: cutting and splitting

A derelict spawns as one `RigidBody3D` with all part collision shapes as children.
When a cut point is severed, the graph edge is removed and connected components are
recomputed. Each component becomes its own `RigidBody3D` with:

- mass = sum of part masses, centre of mass from part positions,
- linear velocity = parent's velocity at the component's new centre of mass
  (parent linear plus angular cross offset), angular velocity = parent's angular
  velocity.

Momentum is conserved. A piece cut off a spinning wreck flies off tangentially, as it
should. This is the moment the game happens.

M2 uses imported convex collision as direct children of each component body. For
consistent split inertia, each part's mass is approximated as a solid box using
its model bounds. Full rotated tensors and parallel-axis terms determine the
component's principal axes and moments, which are explicitly set on the rigid
body. Applying the same model before and after cuts preserves linear and angular
momentum, and kinetic energy, for an isolated split. Shape contact remains convex.
Part poses and condition persist through every split. A grapple on a replaced
body releases; attach again to the freed component.

The practice wreck has eight parts joined at imported sockets with 0.12 m gaps:
two spine sections, a nose, an engine, fuel tank, radiator, antenna, and shield.
It starts with angular velocity (0, 0.06, 0.025) rad/s. The shield blocks some
approaches to the drive section; there are accessible routes to every joint.

## Tools

Powered tools draw suit battery. Physical grips and magnetic soles provide contact
with wrecks and ship interiors; hauling obeys the combined mass and inertia.

| tool | what it does | limits |
|---|---|---|
| Cutter | Sever a cut point you are aiming at within range. Progress bar; time scales with material thickness and cutter power. | Range 8 m. Heat: overuse forces a cooldown. Cutting near a hazard triggers it. |
| Grapple | Tether to a surface or object. Reel in to pull yourself to it, or pull it to you if it is lighter than you. | One tether at a time. Max length 30 m. |
| Hands | Hold a nearby surface, remain attached while cutting, and carry the freed part with suit thrust. | Reach 2 m, finite catch and holding loads; release preserves motion. |
| Scanner | Reveal part kinds, values, masses, and hazard markers within range. | Range 20 m. Takes a few seconds; you must hold still. |

Later tools (not first version): free-cut plating, a deployable tow rig, a demolition charge.

M1 grapple: left click attaches to the nearest aimed surface; right click releases.
Hold R to reel in or T to reel out at 2 m/s, between 1 and 30 m of cable. A central
suit harness pulls both bodies with equal opposing forces, capped at 300 N; the
surface anchor follows the target and off-centre hits apply torque to it. A fixed
wall pulls only the suit. Tension uses a 200 N/m spring and 50 N·s/m damping;
slack cable cannot push. These values are exposed for playtesting.

A successful attachment costs 100 J. The reel draws 1,000 W while moving cable,
with proportional travel on the final partial battery charge and no draw at an
end stop. The passive tether draws no power, so battery depletion prevents new
attachments and reeling but preserves an existing tether. Releasing it preserves
momentum and costs nothing. Escape or loss of focus stops the reel and cancels
pending shots. The cable releases if an obstacle crosses it, its target disappears,
or separation exceeds 30 m. The M1 visual is a straight line, including when slack;
rope sag and wrapping are not simulated.

M2 tool tuning and controls:

- Keys 1/2/3/4 select grapple/cutter/hands/scanner. Capture clicks, Escape,
  focus loss, and switching tools cannot carry a held trigger into a new action.
- Cutter: hold left click on a visible gold marker within 8 m. Both sides of a
  joint are selectable. Cutting time is thickness / 4 mm/s, with a 0.6 multiplier
  for aluminium and a 0.4 s minimum. It draws 1,200 W while cutting. Heat grows
  by 0.24 per powered second; idle cooling removes 0.18 per second. At full heat
  it must cool below 0.25 before cutting resumes. Progress stays on the joint.
- Scanner: 150 W for a two-second scan, revealing parts within 20 m. Suit speed
  must be at most 0.2 m/s, spin at most 0.1 rad/s, and aim steady. Motion or release
  resets incomplete progress. A completed held scan spends no further energy.
  Names/hazard labels stay compact in the world; aiming at a revealed part shows
  its kind, mass, condition, value, and hazard information on the HUD.

Powered work scales to the final fraction of battery energy available. Empty
batteries stop cutter and scanner without modifying existing velocities.

## Hazards

| kind | trigger | effect |
|---|---|---|
| fuel | cutting the tank or a line while not empty | a vent thrust on the tank for several seconds; large tanks can detonate |
| coolant | cutting a radiator line | a spray that shoves nearby bodies, including you |
| pressure | cutting into a sealed hab | the compartment pops: a burst impulse on the part and neighbours, debris |
| power | cutting a live line or the reactor mount | arcs damage your suit and tools; must be shut down first via a panel on the wreck |

Hazards are physical. They push things. A vented tank is a rocket now. This is how
you accidentally disable your own ship.

M2 implements coolant and fuel. Starting a cut on the volatile part's own endpoint
ruptures its line immediately; selecting the neighbouring hull endpoint avoids
that leak. Each part/kind has one finite reservoir. Coolant applies 400 N for four
seconds and costs 0.15 condition; fuel applies 1,000 N for six seconds and costs
0.25 condition. Recoil acts at the outlet, following the part even after a split.
The first other body in the four-metre plume ray receives the exhaust force; walls block
it. Plumes fade and disappear when spent, and cannot be retriggered for free fuel.
The M2 vent approximation keeps rated part mass fixed over its short pulse;
escaping exhaust carries the unabsorbed momentum. Pressure/power hazards remain M6.

## Suit

Oxygen (minutes), propellant (for suit jets), battery (for powered tools, reaction
wheels and boot steps). Oxygen and refilling remain later work.

The fully supplied suit is 100 kg, including 8 kg of propellant, with 720,000 J
(200 Wh) of battery energy. Translation uses up to 180 N. Alt brakes drift and
rotation with up to 600 N / 60 N·m of jets, spending impulse / 2,000 m/s of
propellant (torque uses a 0.5 m effective lever arm). Empty tanks preserve motion.

Normal mouse movement requests a suit-body turn through electrically powered
reaction wheels. The camera stays centred on the torso, and a fast swipe keeps
its full requested turn while the body accelerates and brakes within its torque
and momentum limits. There is no artificial turn-speed cap. Hold Z to move only
the head within ±60° yaw and ±50° pitch; releasing it snaps
the head back to centre. Q/E roll uses the same wheels. They provide up to
50 N·m and store up to ±100 N·m·s on each axis. The wearable module follows Conrad's design
note: 0.0239 kg·m² rotor inertia, about 40,000 rpm at capacity, and a 15 kg
assembly included in the
existing fully supplied 100 kg suit. Each full wheel stores about 209 kJ.
Motors pay electrical/mechanical losses; saturation prevents further momentum
storage. Slowing rotors recovers electrical energy with losses, without deleting
angular momentum. X brakes rotation with wheels only, leaving drift and fuel
alone. Alt unloads stored wheel momentum with opposing propellant jets while
braking drift and spin.

Hold C to run the wheels toward zero momentum. The equal and opposite torque
acts on the suit, so a free suit spins up. A hand grip, seat harness or magnetic
soles transfers that torque through physical contact to the attached body, whose
larger combined inertia produces a slower spin. No wheel system reads or writes
another wheel's storage. If the carrier independently has working attitude
control enabled, its controller reacts to the resulting motion using its own
wheel capacity and battery. Release C to stop unloading. Dumping uses no
propellant and does not guarantee that you or the carrier will stop rotating.

G toggles a hand grip with any tool selected; slot 3 also offers left-click grab
and right-click release. A grip is a live six-axis physical constraint, with no
pose or velocity reset. Catching a spinning wreck conserves angular momentum;
the inelastic catch can dissipate kinetic energy. The combined rotational
inertia slows the shared spin. Holding Alt acts on both bodies through the suit's
finite jets. Large loads can exceed the grip or exhaust the suit's fuel.

The grip follows the selected part through cutting. Hold Z to aim the cutter
while holding; Shift + mouse deliberately steers the combined
load using reaction wheels, and Q/E rolls it. The carried part retains its collisions and
must fit through the cargo door. Release it inside the bay before clamps secure it.
The grapple remains the longer-range tether; the tractor beam has been removed.

B latches magnetic boots when aligned soles are in contact with a designated steel
deck at low relative speed. WASD then walks along the deck in the torso's forward/right directions and
normal mouse yaw turns the torso through the feet. Free look moves the head
without steering the feet and supplies vertical aiming while latched. Engagement
costs 50 J; steps/turns spend battery, with equal reaction on the deck. Foot pivots
have 250 N·m of motor torque and budget 500 J per commanded radian; they retain
the complete turn request without a fixed speed cap. A switchable magnetic latch holds
passively without idle draw; B mechanically releases even with an empty battery.
Holding force and torque are limited; losing deck contact or an excessive load
releases the soles. Boots supply no artificial gravity or remote attraction.

## Extraction: the core trade-off

Your ship has a cargo door of a fixed size and a bay of a fixed volume. A part goes in
whole only if its bounding box fits the door. Otherwise:

- cut it smaller at non-cut-point locations (later feature), losing value,
- tow it outside on the hull, adding mass and drag on your delta-v budget and
  forbidding high warp,
- leave it.

Reaching a part can require removing others first. Removing them can trigger hazards.
Every wreck is a puzzle in cut order, with your fuel and oxygen as the clock.

## Value

`value = value_cr * condition * intact_factor`. Parts damaged by hazards, collisions,
or your own cutter lose condition. Station demand multiplies value on sale.

M2's debug tally sums only individually freed parts, at their current condition;
it does not create credits or imply delivery. Ruptures cause the losses above.
An impact over 2,000 J reduces every part in the struck component by
`min(0.5, (impact_energy_j - 2000) / (component_mass_kg * 300))`; gentle contacts
do not damage it. New fragments have 0.2 s of grace against their siblings so
creating a split does not itself count as an impact. Values remain bounded at zero.
