# Wayfarer (working title)

First-person hard sci-fi salvage game. Fly a small ship between stations and
derelicts with real orbital mechanics, float around wrecks in zero-g cutting them
apart with beam tools, haul what fits, keep your own ship alive, pay your debt.
Single-player. Built in Godot 4.7 with GDScript; ship parts generated by Blender
scripts. Headed for Steam.

**Status:** M2 salvage practice is playable. Scan a spinning eight-part wreck,
cut its connections, move the pieces with a grapple or tractor, and watch the
remaining salvage value. Opening a fuel or coolant line makes the wreck move and
reduces the part's condition. The generated kit contains twelve parts. Next is
M3's player ship in [docs/09-roadmap.md](docs/09-roadmap.md).

## Run

```
./check.sh              # import assets + run all tests headless
godot --path godot      # run the game
godot --path godot -e   # open the editor
```

Needs Godot 4.7.2 on PATH (or `GODOT=/path/to/godot`) and Blender 5.2 for the
part scripts. Setup details in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).

## Controls

- **WASD:** forward/back and sideways thrust relative to your suit.
- **Space / Ctrl:** up/down thrust. **Q / E:** roll left/right.
- **Mouse:** turn the whole suit, including past vertical and upside down.
- **Hold Alt:** brake drift and spin. Release to coast; braking uses propellant.
- **1 — Grapple:** left click attaches, right click releases; hold **R / T** to
  reel in/out. One cable, up to 30 m. Light parts move toward you more easily;
  heavy parts and walls haul you toward them.
- **2 — Cutter:** hold left click on a gold cut point within 8 m. Watch progress
  and heat. The tool cools while idle and locks out temporarily if overheated.
- **3 — Tractor:** hold left click to pull, right click to push, within 15 m.
  Its equal reaction force pushes and can turn your suit too.
- **4 — Scanner:** hold left click for two seconds while still. Hold Alt to
  settle first. Reveals part names and hazard markers within 20 m; aim at a
  scanned part to read its kind, mass, condition, and value on the HUD.
- **F12:** save the game view and HUD as a PNG in `godot/build/screens/`.
- **Escape:** release the mouse; left click captures it again without firing.
  Losing focus releases controls, while the world continues moving. Release a
  held mouse button after changing tools before using the new tool.

## Try a salvage job

Scan first, then fly around the wreck to find its gold connection markers. Each
joint has a cut point on both parts. Cutting the hull side frees its neighbour
without opening that neighbour's volatile line. Cutting on the fuel tank or
radiator starts an orange fuel vent or cyan coolant spray immediately, even before
the joint is fully severed. These jets recoil and can shove anything in their path.
The shield obstructs some approaches; moving around it or removing it first helps.

A part only separates when its last connection is cut. Fragments inherit the
wreck's movement, including sideways motion from spin. Use the grapple and tractor
to manage that motion. When a part is individually freed, its remaining value is
included in **SALVAGED VALUE**. This is a debug tally, not a sale or cargo delivery.
Impacts and ruptured lines reduce condition and value; a safe cut preserves them.

The suit starts with **8 kg of propellant** and a **200 Wh tool battery**. Thrust,
roll, and braking use propellant; coasting and mouse aiming use none. Fuel leaving
the suit reduces its mass. Empty tanks leave you drifting without thrust or brakes.
All powered tools share the battery. Holding or releasing a grapple tether uses
no power, so a dead battery leaves an existing tether intact. Other tools stop
when power runs out. Ship refilling and oxygen arrive in later milestones.

Screenshots use unique timestamped names. A brief HUD message confirms the save or
reports a failure; the console prints the full path. F12 also works with the mouse
released. Captures are local development files ignored by git.

## Generate the part kit

```
blender -b -P tools/blender/make_part_kit.py -- --out godot/assets/models/parts
./check.sh
```

The twelve assets cover hulls, caps, tanks, engines, radiators, a mast, and plating.
Their scripts are the source of truth; each imported model includes convex
collision, sockets, cut points, and physical metadata. The original M1 debris
field remains available as a reusable scene and in the physics tests.
